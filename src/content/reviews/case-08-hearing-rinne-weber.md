## Medical notes

- This case had the highest-priority inconsistency from the earlier sanity check: the old checklist mixed "Weber louder on the right" with "negative left Rinne" and concluded simple left conductive hearing loss. That combination is unstable.
- Simple left conductive hearing loss should usually have Weber lateralizing to the left and Rinne negative on the left.
- Left sensorineural hearing loss usually has Weber lateralizing to the right with Rinne positive bilaterally, though overall perception is reduced on the affected side.
- A negative Rinne can be false negative in profound unilateral sensorineural loss because bone conduction crosses to the opposite cochlea. Contradictory Weber/Rinne findings should be repeated and confirmed by formal audiometry.
- The original task gives symptoms but not the patient's actual test responses, so the educational answer now gives a conditional interpretation matrix rather than inventing a fixed result.
- Additional work-up should include ENT exam/otoscopy, pure-tone audiometry with air and bone conduction, speech audiometry, tympanometry and acoustic reflexes. MRI of the internal auditory canals/cerebellopontine angle is relevant for asymmetric sensorineural loss or neurologic findings.
