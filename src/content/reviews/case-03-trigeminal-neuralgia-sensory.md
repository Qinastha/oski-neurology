## Medical notes

- The symptom pattern fits trigeminal neuralgia: recurrent unilateral brief electric shock-like facial pain in the trigeminal distribution, triggered by innocuous stimuli.
- The case likely localizes to left V2 if the pain is mainly in the cheek/upper jaw region.
- Sensory testing is still required by the station, but persistent objective sensory loss is not a typical confirmation of classic trigeminal neuralgia; it raises concern for secondary trigeminal neuralgia or another trigeminal neuropathy.
- MRI focused on the trigeminal nerve/root and cerebellopontine angle is appropriate to exclude tumor, multiple sclerosis, inflammatory lesions, or other secondary causes. MRA can help assess neurovascular conflict when clinically relevant.
- First-line pain treatment is usually carbamazepine or oxcarbazepine after contraindication and safety review.
