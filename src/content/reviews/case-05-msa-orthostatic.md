## Medical notes

- Orthostatic hypotension is defined as a sustained SBP fall of at least 20 mmHg or DBP fall of at least 10 mmHg within 3 minutes of standing.
- Proper bedside assessment should include BP and heart rate after supine rest and again standing, commonly at 1 and 3 minutes. The station says not to account for time frames, so the UI answer states that real waiting can be simulated while the correct time points are verbalized.
- Neurogenic orthostatic hypotension is supported by an inadequate HR response, such as a low ΔHR/ΔSBP ratio (<0.5 bpm/mmHg) or a small HR rise, but this can be confounded by beta-blockers, rhythm disease, pacemaker dependence and other medications.
- The scenario supports probable MSA: rapid progression, parkinsonism/rigidity, gait instability, urinary urgency and neurogenic OH. A single orthostatic test does not by itself confirm MSA.
- Management should prioritize fall prevention, medication review, fluid/salt expansion when safe, compression/abdominal binder, head-up sleeping, trigger avoidance and specialist-guided medications such as midodrine, fludrocortisone or droxidopa with monitoring for supine hypertension.
