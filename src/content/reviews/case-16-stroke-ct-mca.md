## Medical notes

- The clinical syndrome fits acute left MCA ischemic stroke: right hemiparesis, expressive/motor aphasia with preserved comprehension, onset 3 hours ago, NIHSS 14.
- Atrial fibrillation makes a cardioembolic mechanism likely, but the station should still phrase this as likely/probable unless vascular and cardiac workup confirms it.
- Warfarin with unknown INR is a key decision point. INR must be checked urgently; INR > 1.7 is a contraindication to IV thrombolysis.
- Noncontrast CT is required first to exclude hemorrhage and identify early ischemic signs. The teaching screenshots support hyperdense MCA, loss of gray-white differentiation/insular ribbon and sulcal effacement.
- CTA is important because NIHSS 14 and MCA syndrome raise concern for large-vessel occlusion; if LVO is present, mechanical thrombectomy route should be activated.
- The side is best stated as left MCA by combining imaging teaching context with clinical localization. The screenshots do not include reliable orientation markers, so the answer should not pretend the side is derived from the screenshots alone.
- Supportive stroke-unit care includes BP, glucose, temperature and rhythm monitoring, dysphagia screening before oral intake, DVT prevention, complication prevention and early rehabilitation.
