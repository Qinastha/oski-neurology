# Case 20: cervical-myelopathy-mri

## Verdict

Medically checked. The station now covers history-taking, preliminary diagnosis, correct neuroimaging choice, MRI interpretation and escalation to neurosurgery. The answer explicitly separates radiculopathy-level findings in the arms from cervical spinal cord myelopathy.

## Original task coverage

- History and life history: covered. The checklist includes neck pain, bilateral arm symptoms, Lhermitte-like electric pain, hand dexterity, gait/balance, bladder/bowel symptoms, physiotherapy/manipulation trigger and mimics.
- Preliminary diagnosis and imaging choice: covered. The answer identifies compressive cervical myelopathy and orders cervical spine MRI with sagittal/axial T1/T2 sequences; contrast and extra levels are conditional.
- Imaging interpretation: covered. The MRI is described as central C5-C6 disc herniation/protrusion with canal stenosis, reduced CSF space, ventral cord compression and possible T2 intramedullary signal.

## Medical sanity notes

- The vignette has strong myelopathy evidence: gait aid, bilateral Hoffmann/Babinski, clonus, hyperreflexia in legs, hand dexterity change and possible bladder/bowel symptoms.
- Arm hyporeflexia/atrophy can coexist as a segmental or radicular component at the compressed cervical level.
- MRI is the key test for suspected cervical myelopathy; CT/CT myelography are adjuncts when MRI is contraindicated or bony/OPLL detail is needed.
- Moderate/severe or progressive myelopathy warrants urgent spine/neurosurgical assessment for decompression.
