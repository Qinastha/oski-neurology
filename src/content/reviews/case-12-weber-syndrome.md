## Medical notes

- The case is consistent with Weber syndrome: ipsilateral right oculomotor palsy and contralateral left central hemiparesis.
- Topically this localizes to the right ventral midbrain/cerebral peduncle, involving the right oculomotor fascicles or nucleus-fascicular complex plus corticospinal tract.
- The earlier wording "right III nucleus" was too narrow. A fascicular/ventral midbrain formulation is safer and anatomically cleaner.
- The vascular territory is best stated as right posterior circulation, especially right PCA / paramedian mesencephalic or peduncular perforators, with possible basilar-tip perforator involvement.
- Because symptoms were noticed after awakening, this should be treated as wake-up stroke / unknown exact onset until last-known-well is clarified. MRI DWI/FLAIR can help selection, but urgent CT/CTA stroke pathway should not be delayed.
- Treatment is emergency stroke-unit care with reperfusion eligibility assessment, not outpatient MRI. Atrial fibrillation requires secondary prevention planning, but acute anticoagulation must follow imaging and stroke-team timing.
