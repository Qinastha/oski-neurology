## Medical notes

- The case is highly consistent with cauda equina syndrome: severe low back pain radiating to both legs, progressive bilateral weakness, hypotonia, hyporeflexia, saddle sensory loss and bladder/bowel dysfunction.
- Pelvic dysfunction is best described as peripheral/areflexic sphincter dysfunction from sacral root involvement. In real practice, urinary retention or loss of bladder sensation can be as important as incontinence.
- Topically, the lesion is at the cauda equina nerve roots below the conus medullaris in the lumbosacral canal. Sacral roots S2-S4 explain perineal sensation and bladder/bowel involvement.
- This is an emergency pathway: immediate lumbosacral MRI, urgent spinal/neurosurgical consultation and decompression when compressive CES is confirmed with weakness or sphincter dysfunction.
- CT myelography is a fallback when MRI is unavailable or contraindicated, not the first choice.
