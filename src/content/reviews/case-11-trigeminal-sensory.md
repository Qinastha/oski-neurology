## Medical notes

- The case matches trigeminal neuralgia: unilateral brief shooting facial pain triggered by innocuous stimuli such as washing, tooth brushing and chewing.
- The original task does not specify the exact trigeminal branch, so the safest station answer is right-sided trigeminal neuralgia with branch localization by pain area or exam findings.
- Classical trigeminal neuralgia usually has a normal neurological examination. Objective sensory loss, corneal reflex loss, bilateral symptoms, young age of onset or other neurological signs should raise concern for secondary trigeminal neuralgia/trigeminal neuropathy.
- Sensory examination should compare right and left V1, V2 and V3 territories with light touch and pain, then carefully palpate the trigeminal exit points.
- MRI brain focused on the trigeminal nerve/root entry zone and cerebellopontine angle is a follow-up investigation when confirming etiology or looking for secondary causes.
