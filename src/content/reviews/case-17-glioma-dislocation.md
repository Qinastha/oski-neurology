## Medical notes

- The station fits a large right supratentorial intra-axial mass with perifocal edema, heterogeneous/ring enhancement, necrotic-cystic components, mass effect and midline/ventricular distortion.
- The clinical deficit supports right hemispheric localization: left central hemiparesis/spasticity, left pyramidal signs, reduced GCS and cognitive/speech changes.
- The leading syndrome should be dislocation/mass-effect syndrome from an intracranial space-occupying lesion, with a superimposed left pyramidal syndrome.
- The preliminary imaging diagnosis can be high-grade glioma / suspected glioblastoma, but final diagnosis requires tissue and molecular markers. Do not state a definitive histology from MRI alone.
- Hemorrhage is part of the teaching label/source case. It should be confirmed on SWI/T2* or source sequences; the overview screenshots alone should not be overread as definitive proof of hemorrhage.
- Urgent neurosurgical/neuro-oncology hospitalization is warranted because of GCS 14, progressive deficit, mass effect, midline shift and herniation risk.
