## Medical notes

- The scenario supports probable BPPV: brief positional vertigo triggered by turning in bed and head movements.
- Epley is a canalith repositioning maneuver for posterior canal BPPV. It should be performed for the affected side; the original task does not specify right or left, so the educational answer now avoids inventing a side and says to determine/confirm it by history and Dix-Hallpike.
- The maneuver sequence is: head 45 degrees toward the affected ear, supine with neck extension, head 90 degrees to the opposite side, body roll toward that side with face angled down, then slow return to sitting.
- Holding each position for about 30-60 seconds or until vertigo/nystagmus settles is acceptable for OSCE-style teaching.
- Contraindications or precautions include unstable/severe cervical spine disease, recent neck/head trauma, marked neck pain/limited motion and vascular concerns. Red flags should trigger urgent evaluation rather than repeated home maneuvers.
- Long-term vestibular suppressants or "vascular" drugs are not the core treatment for BPPV.
