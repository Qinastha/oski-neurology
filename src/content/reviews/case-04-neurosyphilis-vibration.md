## Medical notes

- The station pattern is classic for tabes dorsalis: previous syphilis, sensory ataxia worse in darkness, positive Romberg, loss of reflexes, lightning pains, Argyll Robertson pupils, reactive serum tests and CSF abnormality.
- Vibration sense should be tested with a 128 Hz tuning fork over bony prominences, starting distally and moving proximally when distal perception is absent.
- The syndrome is sensory ataxia due to posterior column involvement; areflexia and lightning pains are better explained by posterior root involvement as well, so the answer should not over-localize to only the posterior columns.
- Caveat: real-world neurosyphilis diagnosis relies on compatible neurologic signs plus reactive serology and CSF evaluation. The case gives RPR/TPHA and raised CSF protein but not CSF-VDRL/cell count, so the safest wording is "probable late neurosyphilis / tabes dorsalis, requiring confirmatory CSF pathway."
- Management should include infectious disease/dermatovenereology involvement, HIV testing, CSF confirmation, IV aqueous crystalline penicillin G 18-24 million units/day for 10-14 days under specialist care, follow-up RPR titers, fall prevention and rehabilitation.
