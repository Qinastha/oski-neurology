## Medical notes

- The case fits left ulnar neuropathy at the elbow/cubital tunnel: paresthesia in the ulnar hand, symptoms worse at night and with prolonged elbow flexion, plus hand weakness/fine-motor difficulty.
- Testing should include sensory mapping of the ulnar digits and dorsal ulnar hand, inspection for hypothenar/interosseous atrophy, finger abduction/adduction strength, Tinel at the cubital tunnel and elbow flexion or flexion-compression provocation.
- Dorsal ulnar hand sensory involvement and elbow-level Tinel support cubital tunnel over isolated Guyon canal. C8-T1 radiculopathy, plexopathy and medial epicondylosis remain differentials if the pattern is atypical.
- ENMG/nerve conduction studies confirm site and severity and help distinguish mononeuropathy from radiculopathy/plexopathy. Ultrasound can assess nerve cross-sectional area, swelling, subluxation and structural compression.
- Conservative care is first-line for mild-to-moderate cases without marked weakness/atrophy: avoid pressure and prolonged flexion, nighttime splinting, elbow padding, ergonomic changes and therapy. Progressive weakness, atrophy, severe electrodiagnostic changes or failed conservative therapy warrant surgical evaluation.
