# Case 19: als-mri

## Verdict

Checked with caveats. The original task frames the station as ALS neuroimaging, but the clinical vignette mostly documents upper motor neuron signs and pseudobulbar syndrome. The revised answer therefore keeps ALS as the leading working diagnosis while explicitly requiring lower motor neuron confirmation by examination and EMG/NCS.

## Original task coverage

- History and life history: covered. The checklist now includes progression, bulbar symptoms, respiratory symptoms, lower motor neuron symptoms, family history, cognition/behavior and mimics.
- Preliminary diagnosis and imaging choice: covered. MRI brain plus cervical spine is required to exclude structural mimics; thoracic spine/contrast are added according to clinical suspicion.
- Imaging interpretation: covered. The visible teaching signs are described as corticospinal tract hyperintensity and "wine glass sign", with a clear warning that MRI signs are supportive, not diagnostic criteria.

## Medical sanity notes

- ALS is a clinical-electrophysiologic diagnosis with progressive upper and lower motor neuron involvement and no better alternative explanation.
- Current vignette has strong UMN/pseudobulbar evidence but insufficient LMN evidence. This must be actively asked/examined and confirmed with EMG.
- MRI is mainly to exclude mimics such as cervical myelopathy, tumor, syrinx, demyelinating disease, myelitis and other structural/inflammatory causes.
- CST hyperintensity can support pyramidal tract involvement, but it is subtle and nonspecific.
