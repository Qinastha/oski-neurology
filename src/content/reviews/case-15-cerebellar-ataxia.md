## Medical notes

- The case fits cerebellar ataxia: wide-based staggering gait, coordination difficulty, preserved strength, no sensory deficit and no pathologic reflexes.
- Romberg should not be framed as a positive cerebellar sign. Cerebellar ataxia is unstable even with eyes open; marked worsening only after eye closure points more toward proprioceptive/sensory ataxia.
- The original task explicitly asks for the heel-knee-shin test, so it should be shown as a first-class checklist item, not as optional additional text.
- Localization should be phrased as cerebellum or cerebellar pathways. Gait/truncal instability suggests midline cerebellar/vermis involvement; appendicular dysmetria suggests cerebellar hemispheric involvement, but the case does not justify a precise etiologic diagnosis.
- Progressive adult ataxia warrants a systematic search for treatable causes: structural, vascular, inflammatory, infectious, toxic/drug-related, metabolic/nutritional, paraneoplastic and hereditary causes. MRI brain and neurologic referral are central; rapid or acute progression requires urgent evaluation.
